# 9. Limitations

## 9.1 Cultural validity

The ten dimensions are a useful organization of the problem, but they are researcher-designed. Their boundaries overlap, and the rubric does not represent all cultural perspectives. A score should not be interpreted as an objective judgment about an entire community.

## 9.2 Source limitations

Search engines may favor widely published or English-language sources. Community practices may be documented unevenly, and an official source may describe institutional rules without representing lived experience. Evidence sufficiency is a judgment made by the system, not a guarantee of completeness.

## 9.3 Model dependence

The verifier relies on one semantic backbone for planning, evidence synthesis, and scoring. An error in an early stage can affect later stages. A temperature setting of zero does not guarantee bit-for-bit deterministic semantic outputs.

## 9.4 Evaluation limitations

The 360 prompts come from selected sources and researcher-designed scenarios. They do not form a representative sample of all cultures or all possible LLM uses. The three corpora also differ in construction and difficulty. The old five-annotator Best-of-4 study cannot serve as a gold reference for the new single responses.

## 9.5 Reproducibility limitations

Saved evidence improves repeatability but does not freeze the external world or every model implementation detail. Provider versions, timeouts, and service behavior may change. Exact code, configuration, and snapshot identifiers are necessary to interpret a rerun.

## 9.6 Ethical limitations

Cultural verification must avoid turning majority norms into rules or suppressing legitimate minority views. The system's judgments should be treated as inspectable assessments that can be questioned, not as final authority over cultural identity or values.
