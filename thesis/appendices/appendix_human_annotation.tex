\chapter{Human Annotation Protocol}
\label{app:annotation}

This appendix records the intended annotation protocol that underlies \cref{sec:human-eval}. It is deliberately separated from the final annotation results. The final survey export must be archived and hashed before automated-system results are analyzed.

\section*{Annotator-facing task}
For each evaluation item, the annotator is shown the prompt followed by four complete candidate responses labelled A, B, C and D. No verifier output, reward-model score, direct-judge decision, provisional annotation, expected issue, benchmark answer or evidence trace is shown.

The required categorical question is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Which response is the most culturally appropriate?}
\end{quote}

The available choices are \textbf{A}, \textbf{B}, \textbf{C}, \textbf{D}, and \textbf{No clear winner}. The final option is intended for cases in which the annotator cannot identify one response as clearly more culturally appropriate than the others; it is not a substitute for skipping an item.

A second required item records confidence on an integer scale from 1 to 5. No verbal interpretation of intermediate values is assumed in the thesis unless the final exported survey shows that explicit anchors were presented to annotators.

\section*{Reference construction}
Five student annotators are planned. Votes are unweighted by confidence. Three or more identical categorical votes define a majority. A majority for A--D yields the corresponding human reference winner. A majority for \emph{No clear winner} yields that category. If no category reaches three votes, the item is retained as an \emph{unresolved human split} for disagreement analysis but excluded from the primary winner-agreement denominator.

\section*{Blinding and data handling}
Annotators are not described as cultural experts unless their actual qualifications warrant that description. The aggregate is called a \emph{human reference judgment}, not cultural ground truth. Response identifiers should be pseudonymous or survey-generated; no personally identifying information is required for the scientific analysis. Candidate order and any survey-side randomization must be recovered from the final export rather than assumed retrospectively.
