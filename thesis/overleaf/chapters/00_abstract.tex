Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to provide information, recommendations, and assistance across culturally diverse settings. Yet a response can be fluent, helpful, and seemingly factual while still misrepresenting local practices, reinforcing stereotypes, overlooking religious or social expectations, or applying assumptions that are inappropriate for the user's context. Assessing such responses is challenging because cultural appropriateness is not a universal property: it depends on context, varies within communities, and often involves distinctions between verifiable facts, social conventions, and contested values.

This thesis presents \textbf{Vericult}, a standalone, evidence-grounded framework for the post-hoc evaluation of culturally situated LLM outputs. Vericult evaluates individual prompt--response pairs using a ten-dimensional cultural framework. It identifies context-relevant claims and recommendations, formulates neutral verification questions, retrieves external evidence where appropriate, and separates evidence construction from later response assessment. The system combines source-grounded verification with explicit scoring criteria, uncertainty handling, abstention, and structured diagnostic traces. A controlled LIVE/REPLAY mechanism preserves retrieved evidence for reproducible analysis.

The final experiment uses 360 fixed prompt--response pairs from three collections: PLT120, External120, and Redteam120. One fixed generator produces the responses, after which Vericult evaluates every pair independently. Evidence is collected in LIVE mode, audited, and then reused in REPLAY mode, which is the primary Vericult execution reported in this thesis.

The contribution of this work is an inspectable method for cultural evaluation rather than a claim that external evidence can settle every cultural question. Vericult is designed to make the basis, scope, and limitations of its judgments explicit.
