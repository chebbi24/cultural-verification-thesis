\chapter{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{chap:limitations}

\section{Construct Validity}
D01--D10 are an operational design, not a complete theory of culture. The dimensions overlap and other decompositions are possible.

\section{Source and Retrieval Limitations}
Search engines may over-represent widely published, English-language or institutional sources. Lived practices may be documented less clearly than formal rules. Evidence sufficiency is still a model judgment about retrieved documents.

\section{Model Dependence}
One semantic backbone performs several stages. Early errors can propagate. Temperature zero reduces but does not eliminate model variation.

\section{Dataset Limitations}
The 360 cases are selected from external sources and researcher-designed prompts. They are not a representative sample of all cultures or all LLM use cases.

\section{Human Evaluation Limitations}
The older five-person annotation study refers to different Best-of-4 responses. It can be reported as development history but not as final ground truth for the new 360 responses.

\section{Reproducibility Limitations}
Frozen evidence improves repeatability, but provider implementations and external services can change. Reproduction requires exact code, configuration, hashes and saved artifacts.

\section{Ethical Limitations}
A cultural verifier can reinforce stereotypes if it turns majority tendencies into rules. Its decisions should be treated as inspectable assessments, not authority over cultural identity, religion or values.
